Former PhD students

Soumen Roy

PhD in Physics, IIT Gandhinagar, 2014–2020

Academic background. MSc, University of Calcutta.

Soumen’s doctoral research at IIT Gandhinagar focused on gravitational-wave searches, template-bank construction, and the physics of compact-binary inspirals.

After completing his PhD, he was a Postdoctoral Research Fellow at Nikhef, Amsterdam, from December 2020 to August 2024. Since November 2024 he has been a Postdoctoral Researcher at UCLouvain, Belgium.

Post-IITGN research. His subsequent work has included compact-binary environments, tests of gravity with eccentric systems, and environmental effects such as line-of-sight acceleration.

Representative work from his time at IIT Gandhinagar:

  • Unveiling the spectrum of inspiralling binary black holes
  • Effectual template banks for upcoming compact-binary searches in Advanced LIGO and Virgo data
  • Hybrid geometric-random template-placement algorithm for gravitational-wave searches

Selected later work:

  • Compact binary coalescences in dense gaseous environments can pose as ones in vacuum
  • Testing modified gravity with the eccentric neutron star-black hole merger GW200105
  • Line-of-sight acceleration in compact binaries with higher harmonics and eccentricity

Lalit Pathak

PhD, IIT Gandhinagar, 2018–2024

Academic background. Department of Physics and Astrophysics, University of Delhi.

Lalit’s doctoral work developed fast meshfree methods for gravitational-wave parameter estimation and source localization. This included fast likelihood evaluation, network sky localization, and interpolation of numerical-relativity surrogate waveforms.

After IIT Gandhinagar he was at TIFR Mumbai during 2024–2025 and is currently at IUCAA, Pune.

Post-IITGN research. Lalit has continued work on rapid gravitational-wave inference while also moving into compact-binary astrophysics. One recent direction concerns environmental effects on observed signals, including line-of-sight acceleration from a possible tertiary companion and its degeneracy with orbital eccentricity.

See On the Presence of a Tertiary Compact Object in GW190814.

Representative work:

  • Fast likelihood evaluation using meshfree approximations for reconstructing compact binary sources
  • Prompt sky localization of compact binary sources using a meshfree approximation
  • Fast and faithful interpolation of numerical-relativity surrogate waveforms using a meshfree approximation

Abhishek Sharma

PhD, IIT Gandhinagar, 2021–2025

Academic background. Jamia Millia Islamia; university gold medallist.

Abhishek worked on gravitational-wave inference across ground-based, space-based, and decihertz detectors. His doctoral work included template banks for non-standard compact-binary signals, accelerated parameter estimation for LISA sources, and multiband gravitational-wave astronomy.

He is currently a postdoctoral researcher at IUCAA, Pune.

Post-IITGN research. Abhishek has continued developing fast gravitational-wave inference methods while extending his work toward space-based, decihertz, and multiband gravitational-wave astronomy.

Recent examples include Rapid parameter estimation with the full symphony of compact binary mergers using meshfree approximation and IndIGO-D: Probing Compact Binary Coalescences in the Decihertz GW Band.

Representative work:

  • Template bank to search for exotic gravitational-wave signals from astrophysical compact binaries
  • Accelerated parameter estimation of supermassive black-hole binaries in LISA using a meshfree approximation
  • IndIGO-D: Probing Compact Binary Coalescences in the Decihertz GW Band
